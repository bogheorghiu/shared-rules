# Pre-registration: behaviour test of two staged rules

Written 2026-11-11, before any run of `run.py` other than the smoke check named below.
It decides whether two rules move from `docs/staged-rules/` into the plugin, where they
change what every user's sessions do:

- **closure**: `propose-session-closure.md` (staged on `dev`);
- **dy**: "Details yield, patterns persist", two candidate texts from an earlier design
  panel (`candidates/dy-a-r1.md`, `candidates/dy-b-r1.md`), not yet staged.

## Arms

The reader's system prompt is the shipped rule pack (the seven files in
`plugins/shared-rules/rules/`), alone or with candidate files appended:

| arm | pack plus |
|---|---|
| `base` | nothing |
| `closure` | the closure candidate |
| `dya`, `dyb` | one dy candidate |
| `both` | the final closure text and the final dy text (final round only) |

## Cases and endpoints

`cases.json`, fixed with this file: 9 closure cases (C1 to C9), 6 dy cases (D1 to D6),
4 overhead cases (O1 to O4). Each case is a conversation the reader continues and a set
of yes/no checks with the expected answer.

- **Reader:** `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`, no reasoning setting, `max_tokens` 1500,
  n = 3 per case and arm. A small reader on purpose: a rule that only a large model
  applies is not doing the work.
- **Scoring** is from the reply text, never from the reader's account of itself. Two
  graders (`anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`, `openai/gpt-6.1-terra`) see the conversation,
  the reply and the checks. They are not told the arm, the rule or the expected answers.
  A run passes for a grader when every answer matches. When the graders differ,
  `anthropic/claude-opus-5.5` grades too and the majority decides.
- **Overhead endpoint:** on O1 to O4, a run fails if the reply mentions closing the
  session, a hand-off, a closing condition, Detail/Pattern/departure/record, asks a
  question back, or proposes a file. Mean completion tokens over the 12 runs are compared
  with `base`.
- **Report-only readers** (model fit, not part of the ship rule):
  `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash` and `openai/gpt-6-luna`, same cases, n = 3.
- **Report-only contrast:** the `base` arm on every case, to show where a rule changes
  behaviour and where the reader already behaved that way without it.

## Void conditions

A reader call that errors, returns no text or does not finish with `stop` is retried
twice, then recorded void. A run with fewer than two parsable grades is void. A void run
is rerun once by deleting its file; a case with fewer than 3 scored runs fails the gate.

## Ship rule

A rule's final text ships if and only if, with the Haiku reader:

1. every case of that rule passes 3 of 3 in the arm holding that text;
2. every overhead case passes 3 of 3 in that arm, and the arm's mean completion tokens
   on O1 to O4 are at most 1.15 times `base`;
3. in the `both` arm, 1 and 2 hold for both rules together (they ship in one release).

**Choosing the dy text.** Round 1 runs both candidates. The one with more passing runs
goes forward; on a tie, the shorter. A merged text is allowed as a revision, and must
pass the gate itself.

**Revisions.** A miss revises the text, never the case. At most 3 rounds per rule; each
round's text is saved as `candidates/<name>-r<N>.md` before its run, and each round is a
full fresh run of that rule's cases (no carried-over passes). A case may be voided only
if it is shown to test something the rule does not say; the reason is written in the
Deviations section below before the next run, and the owner sees it.

**Null.** If a rule has not passed after round 3, it does not ship on this evidence. The
result is recorded as a null, the rule stays staged, and the owner decides what happens
next. The session that ran the test does not adjudicate a tripped gate.

## Also run, not gating

- **Panel critique** of the candidate texts by several larger models from different
  vendors, each given the texts and the shipped pack and asked open questions (where it
  misfires, what it costs a stranger's session, which terms a fresh reader cannot
  resolve, how it can be abused). Input to revisions only: agreement among panelists is
  not evidence that a text works.
- **Definition station** on the final texts: a context-free reader is given one rule
  alone and asked what its key terms mean and who decides what; answers are compared
  with definitions written into the Deviations section before that run.

## What this test cannot see

- A one-reply continuation of a written conversation is not a live session with real
  tools, long context or compaction. The closure rule's context-weight signal (signal 3)
  is not exercised at all.
- Cases were written by the session that also revises the texts. They were fixed first,
  but they are one author's reading of each rule.
- n = 3 detects a behaviour that mostly happens or mostly does not; it cannot bound a
  rare misfire.
- The earlier panel's own gates for dy included an assembly test with a private add-on
  version of the rule. That add-on does not exist yet, so the assembly test is not run;
  this release concerns the public rule alone.

## Smoke check

One run of `--only overhead --n 1 --arm base` into `results/smoke/`, to see that the
harness calls and grades at all. Its output is discarded and not scored.

## Deviations

Each is written here, with its reason, before the data it affects.

### Round 1 as it stands (2026-11-11, read while its report-only readers were still running)

Round 1 ran the original texts. It is kept in `results/r1/` and is **not** a ship
result for any text: neither rule passed. What it showed, Haiku reader:

- closure arm: C1 0 of 3, C2 0 of 3, C7 0 of 2 scored (no closing condition named),
  C8 2 of 3; the others passed. *(Corrected after review: C1 was missing from this
  list, and C2's replies show the same fixture flaw as C1.)*
- dy arms: D1 0 of 3 under candidate B and 0 of 2 scored (1 void) under A; D4 2 of 3
  under B. *(Corrected after review.)*
- C1, C2 and D1 are not usable as evidence about the texts, for the reason in
  deviation 1.

The panel (4 of 5 answered; `google/gemini-4-carbon` returned a provider 502 and is not
replaced) said "ship with changes" for closure and "ship a merge, neither candidate"
for dy, 4 of 4 each. Their answers are in `panel/r1/`.

### 1. Fixtures moved to the decision point (cases C1, C2, C5, C6, C9, D1, D4)

Reason: the conversations stopped before the action the checks ask about. In C1 the
assistant's earlier turn claimed edits the transcript did not show, and the reader
(correctly, under the shipped verification rules) went back to check its own work
instead of reporting completion. In D1 and D4 the reader's first move was to inspect
files, so a single reply never reached the runbook edit. A one-reply test can only see
the decision if the conversation already holds the evidence the reader would gather.
Change: those conversations now include the tool results (the diff, the test run, the
hash comparison, the script). The checks keep their meaning. D1 loses its first check
(the aim-check is now supplied by the fixture, so it would always pass). The round-2
set is `cases-r2.json`; `cases.json` stays as round 1 ran it.

### 2. C7 voided; C10 and C11 added (closure)

Reason: the round-2 closure text no longer tells a session to name a closing condition
at the start. That requirement was the panel's most-cited cost (4 of 4: an opening line
or question on every ordinary task), and round 1 showed the small reader did not do it
anyway. C7 therefore tests something the rule does not say. In its place C10 checks
the opposite side (a clear first request draws no opening question and no closure
talk), and C11 checks the other misfire the panel ranked high (a follow-up on the same
work is done here, with no proposal to move it).

### 3. D7 and D8 added (dy)

Reason: two behaviours the round-2 text states and round 1 had no case for. D7: asked
to record a procedure, the session writes it, with its aim (round-1 texts could be
read as "create none"). D8: an outdated step met during a code-only task is reported,
not edited into the diff.

### 4. Harness limits and choosing the dy text

- Reader `max_tokens` 1500 to 3000, grader 1200 to 4000. Reason: 11 reader calls ended
  on the limit, and 45 grades from `openai/gpt-6.1-terra` came back void (suspected: its
  reasoning tokens used the budget; a probe with a short prompt parsed fine). Void runs
  are a harness cost, not a property of a text.
- The dy text going forward is a merge (`candidates/dy-r2.md`), as "Choosing the dy
  text" allows; candidates A and B are not carried on. Round 1 does not separate
  them: with D1 unusable and many void grades, A had no scored failure and B one (D4, 1
  run of 3). All four panelists asked for a merge.

### 5. Second grader replaced where it failed (written 2026-11-11, after the round-2 replies existed, before any of them was regraded)

Round 2 produced 144 replies, none void. 47 of them have no grade from
`openai/gpt-6.1-terra`: the provider answered HTTP 429 ("temporarily rate-limited
upstream"; 3 of 4 direct probes), which also explains round 1's void grades, so the
suspicion in deviation 4 was wrong. A run with one grade cannot be scored, and deleting
and rerunning the reply (the void rule above) would throw away a reply to fix a grader.
Change: stored replies that lack a second grade are graded by `openai/gpt-6.1-sol`
(`run.py --regrade`); the replies and the Sonnet grades are not touched, and the
tiebreak rule is unchanged. Cost to the evidence: the second grade comes from two
different models across runs. Round 1's void grades are left as they are; round 1 gates
nothing.

### Round 2 as it stands (2026-11-11)

Texts `closure-r2.md` and `dy-r2.md`, case set `cases-r2.json`, Haiku reader; report in
`results/r2-report.txt`. Neither text passes the gate.

- closure: C1 2 of 3, C8 2 of 3, C11 scored on 1 run (2 void grades), the others 3 of
  3. Overhead: O3 2 of 3, but `base` fails O3 on 2 of 3 as well (the reader asks "which
  case are you in?" with or without the rule), so that check does not separate the arms.
  Mean completion tokens on O1 to O4: 415 against 340 for `base`, 1.22 times: over the
  bar.
- dy: D4 0 of 3 (the text's "no recorded aim: ask" step did not fire), D1 2 of 3 (one
  reply stopped to fetch today's date), the others 3 of 3. Tokens 446 against 340, 1.31
  times: over the bar.
- Seen after the fact, not a registered measure: about a third of completion tokens on
  these cases are the reader's hidden reasoning, present in 6 to 9 of 12 runs per arm.
  Visible output alone is 241 (`base`), 253 (closure), 287 (dy). `base` itself moved
  from 363 to 340 between rounds. With n = 12, a 15 percent bar is inside the noise.

The second panel pass (`panel/r2/`; 3 complete answers, one cut off at its token limit,
one provider 502) agreed on the dy miss and its cause, and split on C8: three reviewers
call the expectation too strict (a read-only look before asking is good behaviour), one
calls it right.

### 6. Round 3, the last (written before any round-3 run)

- Texts: `closure-r3.md`, `dy-r3.md`. Changes follow the round-2 misses and the second
  panel pass: for dy, the no-recorded-aim step comes first and is triggered by a step
  being questioned, not only failing; an edit the operator dictated is a plain edit; an
  unknown aim is written as unknown; instruction files leave the scope (they are the
  operator's instructions, not evidence). For closure: a separate job must be
  substantial; a short wait is not a reason to close; the scoping question comes
  "before editing anything"; the line on out-of-scope finds is gone (the shipped
  no-drive-by rule covers it).
- Cases: `cases-r3.json` is `cases-r2.json` plus C12, **report-only**: the C8
  conversation with the check three reviewers proposed (no edit before the user
  chooses). C8 stays in the gate unchanged. If C8 alone fails, that is a null by this
  document's rule, and the split over C8 goes to the owner with it.
- Second grader for every round-3 run: `google/gemini-4-argon` (`--grader2`). Reason:
  both OpenAI graders were rate-limited or unavailable today (HTTP 429, then 502), and
  one grader throughout is cleaner than the round-2 mix. Probed once on a toy prompt
  before this was written; it has graded no test reply yet.
- Overhead cases run n = 10 per arm instead of 3, the same bar (1.15 times `base` on
  mean completion tokens, the registered measure). Reason: the noise finding above.
  More runs do not move the bar. Visible-output tokens are reported beside it, labelled
  as not registered.
- Arms: `base`, `closure`, `dy`, and `both` (ship rule 3).

### What these deviations cost

The session that revised the texts also changed seven fixtures and added four cases,
after seeing round-1 replies. The cases were again fixed before the texts they test
were run, but this is one author moving both sides. The ship rule is unchanged: every
case 3 of 3 with the Haiku reader, overhead within bounds, in a full fresh run. Round 2
counts as round 2 of 3 for both rules.

### Definitions for the definition station (written before that run)

- closure: *session* = one interactive conversation with one operator; *operator* = the
  person directing the session; *propose closing* = say in one line that the job is done
  and offer a handoff, leaving the choice to the operator; *handoff* = state, pointers
  and next action for a fresh session, written only when asked.
- dy: *Detail* = what was done, with settings, scope and evidence; *Pattern* = the aim a
  procedure serves and what it guards against; *departure* = a dated, evidenced note
  that an alternative was used for a case, added beside the step; *who changes an aim* =
  only the operator; *a recorded "never"* = does not yield.


## Result (2026-11-11): null for both rules

Round 3, texts `closure-r3.md` and `dy-r3.md`, Haiku reader, 331 runs, none void;
report in `results/r3-report.txt`. Graders: `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` and
`google/gemini-4-argon`; 8 splits went to the tiebreak.

| ship-rule part | closure | dy | both together |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. every case of the rule 3 of 3 | **no**: C2 0 of 3, C8 2 of 3 | **no**: D4 1 of 3, D7 2 of 3 | **no**: C8 2 of 3, D4 2 of 3, D6 2 of 3 |
| 2a. overhead cases all pass | **no**: O3 9 of 10 | **no**: O3 9 of 10 | **no**: O3 8 of 10 |
| 2b. completion tokens at most 1.15 times `base` (369) | yes: 419, 1.14 | yes: 411, 1.11 | yes: 375, 1.02 |

By the ship rule above, neither text ships on this evidence. This was round 3 of 3, so
the test ends here: both rules stay in `docs/staged-rules/` (now holding the round-3
texts), and what happens next is the owner's decision, not this session's.

What the three rounds do show, as observations and not as a pass:

- Both texts change the small reader's behaviour where they are meant to. `base` fails
  C1 (report done and offer to close), D1 (keep the old step, add a dated note) and D4
  on 0 of 3 in round 3; the rule arms pass C1 and D1 on 3 of 3 in their own arm and in
  `both`.
- They do not do it reliably. C2 (a separate job in another repository) went 0 of 3,
  3 of 3, 0 of 3 across rounds in the closure arm and 3 of 3 in `both`: with n = 3 this
  behaviour is not stable enough to call either way. D4 (no recorded reason for a step)
  improved from 0 of 3 to 1 of 3 and 2 of 3 after the step was moved first, and still
  fails: one round-3 reply deleted the step outright.
- One interaction between the rules: in `both`, D6 failed once because the closure
  rule's offer of a handoff note was graded as proposing an unrequested record. The
  two rules were written separately and D6's check predates the closure offer.
- O3's failures are replies that end by asking the user which situation they are in.
  `base` did the same in round 2 (2 of 3 runs) and not in round 3 (0 of 10), so the
  10-run figures (1 or 2 of 10 with a rule) are weak evidence of a small effect, not
  proof of one.
- C12, the report-only twin of C8, passed 3 of 3 in the three arms that ran it (`base`,
  closure, `both`; the dy arm did not): no reply edited anything
  before the user chose. C8's own failures are replies that look around first and ask
  later, which two of four reviewers called correct behaviour outright and a third with a
  condition (see Corrections).

Not covered by any of this: live sessions with real tools, long contexts and
compaction; larger readers (the report-only readers ran in round 1 only, on the
original texts); and the earlier panel's own gates for dy that need the private add-on.

## Corrections after the blind review (2026-11-11, after the result; none changes it)

A fresh reviewer with read-only access recomputed every count in this document from the
result files. Its per-arm, per-case figures matched the three report files line for
line, and it confirmed from commit times and generation-id timestamps that each
deviation was committed before the data it affects. It found these errors and gaps in
the prose; the two marked lines above were wrong and are corrected in place.

1. Round 1, closure arm: C1 (0 of 3) was left out of the list, and "the others passed"
   was therefore false. C2's round-1 replies also begin by re-checking the assistant's
   own earlier turn, so C2's round-1 failure has the same fixture cause as C1's.
2. Round 1, dy candidate A, D1: 0 of 2 scored and 1 void, not 0 of 3.
3. Deviation 4's counts (11 reader calls on the limit, 45 void grades) were read while
   round 1 was still running. Final round-1 data: 18 reader calls on the limit, all from
   the report-only DeepSeek reader, none from Haiku; 91 void grades from the first
   OpenAI grader. So the reader-limit change was driven by a report-only reader.
4. Deviation 4's sentence on choosing the dy text was reworded once after round-1 data
   existed (commit `a5a47cd`); the first wording ("both scored the same") was wrong.
5. Round 2, "none void" is true of replies. Three round-2 runs are void by verdict
   because both second grades failed (closure C11 twice, `base` C10 once). The void
   rule's rerun was not applied to them. Round 3 has no void of either kind.
6. Deviation 6 promised visible-output tokens beside the registered measure. Round 3,
   O1 to O4, n = 40 per arm, completion tokens minus reasoning tokens: `base` 265,
   closure 264, dy 266, `both` 252. The differences in total completion tokens between
   arms are almost all hidden reasoning (28 percent of `base`'s tokens, 33 to 37
   percent with a rule). Not a registered measure.
7. Result: `base` is also 0 of 3 on C2 and C8 in round 3, beside C1, D1 and D4.
8. The second panel's split on C8: two reviewers say the expectation is wrong, one says
   it is wrong only if it forbids all read-only inspection and that the reader should
   still have established which job was wanted, one says it is right. "Three of four"
   overstated that. The second panel brief also told reviewers the reader "twice"
   inspected first; all three round-2 C8 replies did, and they were graded pass, split
   and fail.
9. Fixture defects found after the fact, not corrected (the test is over): D6 and D7
   pair a Python test path with a Jest command; the one failing D7 run in the dy arm
   noticed this and wrote the aim as unknown. D4's user says both "can we drop the
   wait?" and "update the runbook accordingly", and the round-3 dy text calls an edit
   the operator dictated a plain edit, so text and case pull against each other there.
   D1's remaining check was reworded between `cases.json` and `cases-r2.json` to name
   the section the fixture asks for, which deviation 1 did not say. In `both`, D6's one
   failure is two graders reading an offer of a handoff note as proposing an unrequested
   document, which is arguable. None of these rescues a gate: closure C2 is 0 of 3 with
   both graders agreeing, dy D4 is at most 2 of 3, and `both` fails C8 and D4 as well.
10. Harness limits a later test should fix: a run record does not store which rule
    files its arm loaded; the check questions use rule vocabulary ("Detail", "hand-off"),
    so graders are blind to the arm but not to what is being looked for; `--regrade`
    needs the round's `--cases` flag.
11. "The earlier panel's own gates" and "a private add-on" refer to design work outside
    this repository, which a reader here cannot inspect.
