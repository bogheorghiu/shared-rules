Keep the aim, adapt the means — a way of doing something is not what it is for

The failure mode.

The failure mode. Any code, procedure, instruction, config, note or plan is one means to an aim, chosen for the cases its author could see. On a case they did not see (a new model, a new source, a missing tool) both mistakes look like success: the step is followed and no longer does its job, or it is replaced and what it protected goes with it.

"check that the means did its job, not just that it ran" Shown No room to help in the normal setup

How to apply.

On a new kind of case, check that the means did its job, not just that it ran. A check passed: did it look at your file?

"Told … Met … Undoable" Open

What a change leaves behind. Leave whatever form, here, gives all three of these record properties. Told: the user learns what did not fit and what you did instead, briefly, where they already look, as soon as they can and not saved for a wrap-up. Met: any agent that would rely on the old means meets the change before acting, without searching. Undoable: the old way can be restored. For later agents, a means changed where it lives speaks for itself and history keeps the old: add no comment narrating it. A means you leave as written, where the next such case would hit the same miss unnoticed, gets a note beside it, in that file's own style, saying only what you saw it miss; that note is part of the change, not a drive-by. Where nothing keeps history, include the old value. No new file, log or format. If that note belongs where you may not write, or the old way could not be restored, hold that part and ask first.

Proposed replacement text

untested, proposed for a test arm only

When you write a brief: keep what the owner decided apart from what anyone proposed. Say what the reader must be able to find or do, and what must keep working.

When you build: something already in place is also only one way of doing its job. Adding the new thing beside it is a design choice, not a safe default: check what the reader meets first. If the old form gets in the way and you were not told to keep it, change it and say so. If you were told to keep it, say what that costs and ask. Having warned the user does not make a result that fails its purpose done.

The next test, two steps

  1. Small. Run L1 again, unchanged, with one added sentence: "You may change what's there if that serves this better." Eight sessions without the rule. If they now serve the purpose, the gap is permission, not reasoning. A reviewer proposed a probe of this kind and it was never run. DESIGN.md:L1131

  2. Larger. A half-built page and a frozen hand-off brief taken from your real one. One thing varies: the heading over the proposed layout, "Design goals" or "Proposed in discussion, not decided". Phone readers must find the stalled job, what would release it and whom to call. Nothing is hidden. If control passes anyway, stop there. reviewer Reviewer design: Astra, adapted.
