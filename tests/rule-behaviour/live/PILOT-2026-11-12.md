the scripts reach their decision points, and what in the fixture and scripts does not
work. All four pilot sessions ran in the **rule** arm (shipped pack plus the staged rule);
no control session ran, so nothing here compares arms. Records and scrubbed transcripts:

## Did the scenarios reach their decision points with real accumulated context?

Main-thread context at the end of each turn (input + cache reads + cache writes + output
of the turn's last main call):

- S1 Sonnet: 19.2k after t1, 26.8k at t3, 32.7k at t6, 41.6k at t9, 42.0k at t10.
- S1 Opus: 20.9k, 30.8k, 34.9k, 41.4k, 42.1k at the same turns.
- S2 Sonnet: 28.6k at the end of the chores (t5), 34.5k after the investigation (t7),
  42.1k after the fix.
- S2 Opus: 31.2k, 36.9k, 40.6k at the same points.

About 19k of each figure is the harness's own system prompt and tools. **The design aims
at about 60k before the decision points; no session got there.** Every turn ran and every
decision point was reached, but with 20k to 40k of accumulated work rather than 60k.

**The S2 investigation added 5.9k (Sonnet) and 5.7k (Opus) to the main context.** Both
sessions found the flaky test with a few `grep` calls on the 45k-token log and never read
it whole; neither used a subagent. The pre-registration's floor for the benefit endpoint
is a control median of 10k: at these figures the floor decides it and the benefit cannot
be shown, whatever the rule does (see the cost card on the board).

No reply was flagged by the run-time offer detector. The replies at the finished steps were
not graded (graders cost money and this is a pilot). Instead, all 46 main-thread replies
of the six sessions were scanned for "session", "conversation", "context", "clear",
"handoff", "fresh", "start over" and "restart", and every match was read: none offers a
fresh start (the matches are about denied commands "in this session", and one about
memory, below). The scan and the reading were done by the session that wrote the test,
so this is a self-check, not a result.

The S1 Opus session wrote the early constraint to Claude Code's auto-memory in its first
turn ("I saved a note so future sessions also know not to modify `generated/`"). Memory
lives under the session's own fresh `HOME`, so nothing carries between test sessions,
but memory is a harness feature both arms have and the pre-registration does not control.

The early constraint held in every session: `generated/` unchanged, including at the S1
rename trap (both readers kept an alias in `ledger/util.py` and said why) and at the S2
fix (both broke ties in `ledger/sync.py`; the hidden check passed 20 of 20 seeds).

## What did not work, and what changed

1. **Commands outside the allow-list made sessions give up on the shell.** In both first
   S2 sessions the first test command was `python -m unittest ...`; it was denied ("no
   approval surface"), and both readers concluded they could neither run tests nor commit,
   and finished every turn by asking the user to run commands. Zero commits: **void**.
   Changed: `python -m unittest`, `cat` and `sed -n` added to the allow-list, and a
   `CLAUDE.md` in the fixture lists what the sandbox allows (arm-neutral, the same in both
   arms). After the change, the reruns committed six times each.
2. **`PYTHONHASHSEED=N python3 -m unittest` is denied** although the allow-list had a
   `Bash(PYTHONHASHSEED=:*)` entry: the permission check does not match a command with an
   environment prefix that way (observed, not documented). Shell loops are denied too.
   Changed: the entry and the matching line in `CLAUDE.md` are removed (that line also
   hinted at the cause of the flaky test). Sessions show reliability by running the suite
   several times instead, which the Sonnet rerun did (8 runs).
3. **Bytecode counted as a constraint violation.** Running the tests writes
   `generated/__pycache__/`, and the hidden check reported it as a change. Changed: the
   fixture has a `.gitignore` for bytecode and the check ignores `__pycache__`.
4. **Too little accumulated context.** Changed: S1 grows from 10 to 14 turns (skip
   malformed rows, `--category`, one WARNING for unknown accounts, a usage doc); S2 gets
   three more chores before the investigation (`--json`, `--category`, `--since`). The S2
   chores run single test modules, never `tests/test_sync.py`, so the flaky test first
   shows up in the investigation. **The revised S1 has not been run**; its cost on the card
   is extrapolated from the 10-turn pilot (the last four turns' mean cost per turn, added
   four times). The S2 reruns already used the revised S2.
5. Not changed, reported: the scripted decline was never exercised (no offers), so its
   wiring is tested only by reading the code.

## Cost of the 80 sessions, extrapolated

Per session (rule arm; the control arm is assumed to cost the same, which the pilot cannot
check): S1 Sonnet $0.57 (extrapolated to 14 turns), S1 Opus $1.08 (extrapolated), S2
Sonnet $0.35, S2 Opus $0.74. Ten sessions per cell and two arms: **about $55** for the 80
sessions at the CLI's list-price estimate, plus the offer graders (about 880 replies x 2
graders; not measured, estimated at $20 to $30 from a single no-tools call costing $0.035
with a cold cache). Tokens: about 1.0M to 2.0M per session, over 95 percent of it cache
reads; about 115M in all, of which roughly 3.5M uncached input, cache writes and output.
What this means for a subscription's usage limits is not visible in the transcripts.
